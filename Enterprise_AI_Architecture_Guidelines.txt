1. OVERVIEW OF RETRIEVAL-AUGMENTED GENERATION (RAG)
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) optimizes LLM outputs by referencing an authoritative
knowledge base outside of its training data before generating a response.
The standard enterprise pipeline executes in two discrete stages:
  - Stage 1: Dense vector retrieval over FAISS or ChromaDB indices.
  - Stage 2: Context augmentation in the prompt passed to the inference model.
Enterprise benefits include reducing hallucination risk by 84%, providing domain-specific recency
without continuous model re-training, and offering verifiable source citations for corporate audits.

2. VECTOR INDEXING AND MATHEMATICAL SIMILARITY
Document passages are converted into 384-dimensional dense vector embeddings using
sentence-transformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2). FAISS performs k-nearest neighbor retrieval using
cosine similarity and inner product metrics.
Crucially, high semantic similarity is an information retrieval signal and does not mathematically
prove that a statement is true. Claims must be cross-verified by specialized evaluation judges.

3. HALLUCINATION DETECTION AND VERIFICATION STANDARDS
The system classifies claims into four strict categories:
  - Supported: The evidence directly agrees with the statement.
  - Unsupported: No sufficient supporting passage was found; this alone does not prove the claim is false.
  - Contradicted: Reliable evidence conflicts with the claim.
  - Inconclusive: Evidence is missing, ambiguous, or insufficient.
A statement lacking reference evidence is defined as Unsupported and must never be automatically
equated with factual falsehood unless directly contradicted by verified ground truth.
